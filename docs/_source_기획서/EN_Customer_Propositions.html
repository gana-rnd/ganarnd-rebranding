DR. PARK & GANA

Customer groups and value propositions

About this draft. This follows the structure, sequence and wording of Charles Laporte Aust's first draft. Two things have been added: the supporting points under “Why” now carry registration numbers rather than capability statements, and a seventh segment has been proposed at tissue level. The partnership models are unchanged, as are the propositions.

1. AAV Co-development

SEGMENT 1   DR. PARK

WHO IS IT FOR?

Physician-scientists, inventors, specialist laboratories, universities, research institutes and rare-disease organizations.

THEIR PROBLEM

They may have an important gene-therapy invention, but lack the funding, regulatory expertise, CMC capabilities and manufacturing infrastructure required to turn it into a real therapy.

WHAT WE OFFER

A flexible, end-to-end co-development partnership — from early invention and regulatory planning through clinical development and industrial-scale manufacturing.

FROM INVENTION TO SCALE — WITH ONE COMMITTED PARTNER.

WHY DR. PARK?

  • Direct access to the principal decision-maker — the founder, Dr. Yongho Park
  • Selective early investment and co-equity
  • Regulatory and CMC development
  • Plasmid and AAV development — 1,900 L fermentation with a dedicated GMP plasmid downstream suite
  • Purification and analytics — chromatography-led, with Thermo Fisher DYNASPIN harvest
  • Phase 1 viral vector facility completed August 2025; cGMP certification targeted for 2026
  • A defined route from early development through to commercial-scale supply

PARTNERSHIP MODEL

Co-development, cash investment, contributed R&D and manufacturing resources, deferred fees, co-equity, royalties, licensing rights and long-term manufacturing agreements.

WHAT WE OFFER

A licensed medical device manufacturer with wound dressings already registered in multiple jurisdictions — including domestic approval — and the capability to manufacture under a partner's brand.

WE ALREADY MANUFACTURE THE DEVICE YOUR MARKET REQUIRES.

WHY GANA?

Why this segment is proposed. Wound care is the best-evidenced product line in the group and appears in none of the six original propositions. It also serves a different buyer — hospitals and long-term care rather than aesthetic clinics, purchasing on clinical evidence rather than aesthetic outcome. Structurally it is what connects the two brands: it establishes GANA R&D as a licensed medical device manufacturer that also serves aesthetics, rather than a cosmetics supplier that also sells devices.

How the two brands relate — three depths of manufacture

DR. PARK and GANA are not two unrelated businesses that happen to share an owner. They are the same activity carried out at three different depths, defined by what the product acts on and how far into the body it goes.

One manufacturer's name is on both the filler registrations and the wound dressing registrations, and the viral vector facility is on the same site under the same founder. Large groups do span categories, but they do it by acquisition and outsource the production. A competitor can assemble a portfolio that looks like this by sourcing; they cannot get one manufacturer's name onto all of it.

Two further consequences follow.

Partners can move between layers. A clinic buying dermal products today is a candidate for regenerative combinations tomorrow. A distributor holding an aesthetic portfolio is a candidate for wound care in the same territory. A biotech buying process development is a candidate for commercial supply later. The portfolio is a path rather than a catalogue, which is what allows a partnership to compound.

One founder, one site, three depths. GANA R&D and Dr. Park CDMO operate from the same facility at 555 Dunchon-daero, Jungwon-gu, Seongnam, under the same founder. We do not claim a single integrated technology platform spanning all three layers. We do state that the same organisation, on the same site, manufactures at all three — and that is unusual.

A note on how to put it: the point is not that one depth is more advanced than another. A dermal filler is a higher-risk device class than a wound dressing, and anyone in the field will know it. The point is the single manufacturer name across all of it.
